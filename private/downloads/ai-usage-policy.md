This is written to be adopted, not admired. It avoids banning things people
will do anyway, because a policy everyone quietly ignores is worse than no
policy — it just moves the risk out of sight.

---

## 1. Scope

This policy covers use of generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini,
Copilot, and similar) by anyone working at **[ORGANISATION]**, including
contractors.

It does not cover AI features embedded in tools we already approve
(e.g. spell-check, spam filtering), which are governed by our existing
supplier agreements.

## 2. The short version

If you remember nothing else:

1. **Never paste anything you'd be uncomfortable seeing screenshotted in a news story.**
2. **Use an approved account for work.** Personal accounts train on your data.
3. **You own what you send.** "The AI wrote it" is not an explanation for an error.

## 4. What must never be entered into an AI tool

Regardless of tool or account type:

- Passwords, API keys, access tokens
- Customer or employee personal data (names with contact details, addresses, ID numbers)
- Health, financial or biometric data about any individual
- Anything under NDA, legal privilege, or an active dispute
- Unreleased financial results or commercially sensitive figures
- Source code or designs we treat as proprietary

**Redact instead of avoiding.** You rarely need the sensitive specifics.
"The finance director at a mid-size client" works as well as a real name.
Replace figures with rounded or fictional equivalents where the exact number
isn't the point.

## 5. Where AI must not make the decision

AI may assist with preparation. It must not be the deciding factor in:

- Hiring, promotion, discipline or termination
- Credit, pricing or eligibility decisions about a named individual
- Anything with a safety, medical or legal consequence
- Any communication where the human element is the point (condolence,
  apology, recognition)

Where AI assists a decision about a person, a named human records the
rationale and remains accountable for it.

## 6. Verification

Before AI-assisted output leaves the organisation or informs a decision:

- **Check any figure, date, quote, citation or legal reference.** These are
  the categories AI most often invents, and it does so fluently.
- **Apply the consequence test:** *if this is wrong and nobody notices, what
  happens?* Nothing much → proceed. Something serious → verify. Something
  irreversible → a qualified human decides.

Confidence in the output tells you nothing. A fabricated answer reads exactly
like a correct one.

## 7. Disclosure

Using AI is normal and does not need announcing line by line. However:

- **Do not present unreviewed AI output as your own considered work.**
- **Disclose when a client or regulator would reasonably expect to know** —
  for example, AI-generated material in a deliverable they're paying for, or
  in anything submitted to a regulator.
- If asked directly whether AI was used, answer honestly.

Concealment is what turns acceptable use into a credibility problem.

## 8. Data handling

- Use accounts provisioned by **[ROLE/TEAM]**. These are configured so
  conversations are not used to train the providers' models.
- Personal accounts must not be used for work material, whatever the setting.
- Assume anything typed into any AI tool may be retained and may be read by a
  human reviewer at the provider.

## 9. If something goes wrong

If you enter something you shouldn't have:

1. Delete the conversation.
2. Tell **[ROLE — e.g. the Data Protection lead]** the same day.
3. Do not attempt to assess the severity alone.

**Reporting early is expected, not punished.** Late discovery is what turns a
manageable incident into a reportable breach. Nobody has ever been
disciplined here for reporting a mistake promptly.
